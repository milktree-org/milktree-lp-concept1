# Milktree: brand deep dive for campaign briefs

**Version:** 1.0, 6 October 2026
**Purpose:** The single reference document for anyone, human or AI, writing creative briefs for Milktree ad campaigns. Everything here is either live on milktreeagency.com, in the studio's own data, or marked as an opinion. Nothing is invented. If a brief needs a fact that isn't in this document, the brief should say "unverified" rather than make one up.
**Companion files:** `MILKTREE-STUDIO.md` (offer and site spec), `marketing/growth-strategy-research.md` (the evidence behind the repositioning), `lib/offer.ts` (prices, the source of truth).

## 0. How to use this document

When writing a brief:

1. Start from a **symptom** (§8), not a feature. The ads that worked named a visible problem.
2. Pick the **product** that fixes that symptom (§5). One product per ad. Never "three ways to work with us" in a 1080 square.
3. Pick the **sector** if the audience is sector-targeted (§4), and use that sector's named clients as proof.
4. Write in the **voice** (§9) and lay it out in the **visual system** (§10).
5. Send traffic to the **landing page for that product** (§11), never the homepage, with the UTM pattern in §11.3.
6. Check the brief against the **banned list** (§8.6) before it goes out.

A brief that follows those six steps will be on-brand. A brief that opens with "unlimited design requests" or "your creative department" is off-brand and should be rejected.

**Milktree is a UK brand design studio that builds brands for real businesses and takes them all the way to the things people can see: signage, packaging, menus, vehicles, decks, ads and websites.** Seven years, 200+ brands, 15+ industries. **Positioning line:** *Brands you can see on the high street.*

**Why that line.** UK design subscriptions (Hatchly, Studiovine, Design Shake, Berlew) sell digital assets: social posts, ads, decks, flyers. Milktree builds whole brands for businesses whose brand lives on a wall, a van, a menu and a shopfront, and takes the work off the screen into print and outdoor as standard. That is the visible, defensible difference, and the portfolio proves it. Use it.

### 1.1 What changed, and why briefs must reflect it

Until September 2026 the site sold one thing: a design subscription ("your creative department, on demand", £1,999 and £3,999 a month) aimed at marketing teams in 10 to 100 person companies. It produced zero clients from roughly 5,000 cold emails, about £1,600 of Meta ads and 28 inbound leads. The ads got clicks at three to five times the B2B median. Nobody bought, because the people clicking wanted a specific thing fixed, and the page offered an open-ended retainer to a buyer Milktree had no proof for.

The new position sells what 200 businesses have already bought from Milktree, as products with prices, to the owner-led businesses the portfolio actually shows. The subscription still exists. It is the back end of the relationship, not the front door.

**Implication for every brief:** lead with the problem and the fixed-price fix. Treat the subscription as "what happens after", never as the headline.

## 4. Who it's for

### 4.1 Primary buyer

The owner or founder of a UK business with roughly 5 to 50 staff, consumer-facing or local-service, whose brand has fallen behind the business. They arrive from an ad, a cold email, a referral or Instagram. They want to see work that looks like their sector, know what it costs, and talk to a person. They are not a marketing department. They make the decision themselves, often in the evening, on a phone.

**What they feel:** the business has moved on, the brand hasn't. They can't always say what's wrong, but they can recognise it when it's named. Embarrassment is the emotion, not ambition. They've been quoted before and ghosted the quote.

**What they don't want:** a proposal, a discovery call before a price, a retainer with no end, agency language.

### 4.2 Secondary buyer

The single marketer inside that business, who runs the subscription after a build. Reach them with the subscription message only after a build relationship exists, or via retargeting.

### 4.3 Not the buyer

Heads of marketing at 100+ person companies with in-house design volume. No proof, no relationships. Do not write copy for them.

## 5. The offer ladder in full

All prices exclude VAT. All fixed. No proposals, no quotes, no hourly billing anywhere.

### 5.1 Brand Reset Sprint, £799, two weeks

**One thing fixed properly.** The buyer chooses one of three:

| Option | The symptom it answers | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | "The page can't explain what you do in one sentence." | New homepage design, desktop and mobile; headline and section structure; design files for their developer |
| Pitch deck | "The deck doesn't match the website, or the business." | Up to 15 slides restructured and designed; master template; editable in PowerPoint, Keynote or Google Slides |
| Identity tighten-up | "It looks like ten different people made it." | Logo lock-ups, colour and type brought back into line; three key applications; one-page guideline sheet |

**How it runs:** Day 1 brief (30-minute call and questionnaire). Day 3 two rough directions, they pick one. Day 7 first look, revision round starts. Day 10 final, files delivered.

**Every sprint includes:** two weeks start to finish, one revision round, creative director on it, files ready to use, fee credited in full if they step up within 30 days.

**Payment:** paid in full to book. Self-serve checkout exists on `/sprint` when enabled; otherwise a 15-minute call books it.

**Role in campaigns:** this is the front door. Every cold ad should sell the sprint. It is a yes at £799, not a yes at £1,999 a month.

### 5.2 Brand Build, £3,499 fixed, four to six weeks

Positioning workshop (half a day), positioning on a page, two visual directions, full identity (logo system, colour, type, imagery), five core applications, brand guidelines, handover. Named senior designer, creative director throughout. Two revision rounds at each stage.

**Week by week:** 1 workshop and positioning; 2 direction; 3 to 4 identity; 5 applications; 6 guidelines and handover.

**Payment:** 50% to start, 50% on delivery.

### 5.3 Brand Build Plus, £5,999 fixed, six to eight weeks

Everything in Brand Build plus the toolkit to launch it: campaign ad set, social template pack, print and outdoor (signage, packaging or out-of-home as relevant), launch assets. The right product for any business people can see from the street.

### 5.4 Essentials subscription, £1,499 a month

Unlimited requests, one active at a time, around 48 hours on standard requests. Worked by the core team or a vetted designer from the network, every piece checked by a creative director. One revision round per request. Pause or cancel any month. Unused days bank when paused.

### 5.5 Design Lead subscription, £2,499 a month

Unlimited requests, two active at a time. **A named senior designer, the same person every time, direct on the client's Slack.** Creative direction on everything. Pause or cancel any month. The value anchor: a UK design lead costs £65k+ a year before National Insurance, holiday cover and recruitment, and buys one skill set. Design Lead is £30k a year, senior across every discipline, cancel any month.

### 5.6 Hard rules that briefs must respect

- Subscriptions are for ongoing work. **Full brand builds are a product, never something that "happens on" a subscription.**
- "Senior" is reserved for Design Lead's named designer and the Brand Build's named designer. Essentials copy says "vetted designer from our network, checked by a creative director."
- No founding rate, no scarcity offers, no "spots left". Milktree is an established studio.
- "Need more? Let's talk" is the only unpublished tier. Never advertise it.
- The sprint credit is the only discount mechanism. No percentage-off ads.

## 7. What the data says works

From Milktree's own Meta Ads account, February to September 2026, around £1,590 spent across the old subscription positioning. Click-through was 2 to 4 percent on lead campaigns against a B2B paid-social median of 0.65 to 0.80 percent. The creative got attention. The offer didn't convert. Twenty-eight leads, zero clients.

### 7.1 Which hooks produced leads

| Hook (ad name) | Leads | Approx. cost per lead | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Your brand looks unclear" (AWR-01) | 6 | £15 | Best performer. A visible embarrassment, named. |
| "Homepage can't explain you in one sentence" (DES-02) | 5 | £24 | Specific, recognisable, maps directly to the homepage sprint |
| "Pitch deck doesn't match your website" (AWR-02) | 5 | £43 | Maps directly to the deck sprint |
| "Hot take: ten people, ten fonts" (DES-01) | 4 | £30 | The identity tighten-up, in the buyer's words |
| "Cheaper than a full-time hire" (F03) | 1 | £110 | The subscription's core argument. Barely moved. |
| Brand Score quiz | 1 | £36 | Lead magnet. Weak as a cold ad. |

**The lesson:** every hook that worked describes a specific, visible problem with an existing brand. The hook that described the subscription's value produced almost nothing. The new offer ladder was built from this. Cold ads should name a symptom and sell the sprint that fixes it.

### 7.3 Channel plan the research supports

- **Meta:** £30 a day, the "brand looks unclear" family of hooks, straight to `/sprint`, one conversion event (Lead on form submit). Target lead-form completion of 5 percent or better and cost per sprint enquiry of £60 or less.
- **Cold email:** 400 a day, owner-led businesses in the six sectors, observation-led first line, sprint as the ask, never pricing by email. Judge against a 1 percent reply floor at 30 days.
- **Referrals:** the top source of new business for UK agencies. The `/refer` page offers a free sprint or £799 credit for any introduction that becomes a Brand Build. Past clients and partners (accountants, developers, printers, photographers) are the warmest list.
- **Social:** one public brand teardown a week, every completed sprint becomes a before-and-after.

## 8. Messaging

### 8.1 The four symptoms (use the buyer's words)

These produced every lead in 2026. They are the opening line of most ads.

1. **The homepage.** "It can't explain what you do in one sentence." People land, squint, and leave. The business has moved on; the page hasn't. Fix: homepage sprint.
2. **The deck.** "The deck doesn't match the website." Different logo, different colours, different story. It reads like two companies. Fix: deck sprint.
3. **The brand.** "It looks like ten different people made it." Because they did. A freelancer here, a template there, six years of drift. Fix: identity sprint or Brand Build.
4. **The backlog.** "Design is always the thing that's late." The menu, the ads, the signage for the new site, all waiting on a designer who isn't there. Fix: subscription.

Plus the umbrella line that opens the homepage: **"Your business has grown. Your brand hasn't caught up."**

## 9. Voice and tone

**In a sentence:** confident, premium, plain-spoken. Short lines. Lead with the customer's problem and our proof, never our features. Say "you" more than "we".

**Rules**

- Short sentences. One idea each. Full stops, not dashes.
- Plain words. "Fix", "rebuild", "keep us on". Not "transform", "elevate", "unlock".
- Name the problem before the product. The product is the second sentence, never the first.
- Prices in copy, always. Hiding the price is the old behaviour.
- No hype, no exclamation marks, no emoji, no rhetorical questions in a row.
- Honest to the point of bluntness. "If it's all three, that's a brand problem and a Brand Build is the honest answer."
- British spelling. "Colour", "organise", "programme".
- Numbers as numerals in ads (200+, £799, 2 weeks). Spelled out in long copy where it reads better.

**Good:**
> Your homepage can't explain what you do in one sentence. We'll fix it in two weeks. £799, fixed.

**Bad:**
> Unlock your brand's potential with Milktree, your on-demand creative department! Unlimited requests, senior designers, amazing results.

**Good:**
> The deck doesn't match the website. Fifteen slides, rebuilt, with a master template so the next one holds. Two weeks.

**Bad:**
> Need a stunning pitch deck? Our award-winning team transforms your presentations.

## 10. Visual identity for ad creative

### 10.1 Tokens

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Background | #000000 true black | Default canvas for everything |
| Surface | #0B0B0A | Raised bands |
| Card | #141200 warm near-black | Cards, tiles |
| Foreground | #FFFFFF | Headlines, primary text |
| Muted | rgba(255,255,255,0.60) | Body text |
| Faint | rgba(255,255,255,0.52) | Labels, small print |
| Accent | #FFEE02 electric yellow | The one accent. See 10.3 |
| Accent ink | #000000 | Text on yellow |
| Border | rgba(255,255,255,0.12) | Hairlines |
| Radius | 36px cards, full pill buttons | Heavily rounded geometry |

Dark only. There is no light-mode brand. White-background ads are off-brand except where a platform forces it; even then, black type on white with one yellow element, never a grey or pastel palette.

### 10.2 Type

- **Headlines:** Instrument Sans, condensed (width 75), bold, **uppercase**, tight tracking (-0.01 to -0.03em), line height 0.95 to 1.0. Big. Headlines are the design.
- **Body and UI:** Satoshi, regular or medium, 1.0625 to 1.3rem equivalent, line height 1.6, muted white.
- **Eyebrow labels:** 0.8rem, bold, uppercase, tracked 0.18em, with a short yellow tick before them.
- **Prices:** Satoshi bold, large, tight tracking, white. The price is a design element, not small print.

### 10.3 The yellow rule

**One yellow element per frame, maximum.** It marks the single most important thing: the CTA, the price, or one keyword. In a static ad that is almost always the price or the CTA button. Never yellow backgrounds for whole frames, never yellow headlines plus yellow buttons, never yellow as decoration. Yellow is a scalpel, not a paintbrush.

### 10.4 Imagery

- **Real work, photographed in the world.** Signage on a street, a menu on a table, a van on a road, packaging in hands, a billboard with a person walking past. This is the whole positioning made visible.
- Prefer the existing work strip and case study galleries. Compose ads from them.
- Black canvas with the photograph in a rounded card, or full-bleed photograph with a dark gradient and type over it. Both are on the site.
- Before-and-afters once real sprints exist (the component is built; the data file is empty until two sprints have shipped). Never stage a fake before.
- No stock photography of "teams collaborating". No abstract 3D blobs. No AI-generated mock-ups of imaginary brands.
- The founder may appear in About-style creative once a photograph exists. No team grids, no headshots of the network.

### 10.5 Logo

The Milktree wordmark (lowercase "milktree" with the mark) in white on black, or black on yellow. The favicon mark alone for small placements. Never stretched, never coloured, never on a photograph without a dark scrim. Files in `/public/logos/`.

### 10.6 Motion (video and animated placements)

- Slow, eased, restrained. Expo-out easing. One move at a time. "Alive, not animated."
- The signature: headline lines rising out of a clip mask, one after another, 0.09s apart.
- Reveal once; never bounce; never shake; no particle effects.
- Only transform and opacity animate. No zooming photographs. No lens flares.
- 0.6 to 0.9s for reveals. Hold the final frame with the price and CTA.

### 10.7 Formats and safe areas

| Placement | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Feed square | 1080×1080 | Headline up to 8 words, price, CTA. Logo bottom-left small. |
| Feed portrait | 1080×1350 | Preferred for Meta. Room for photo plus three lines plus price. |
| Story / Reel | 1080×1920 | Keep type in the middle 60 percent vertically. CTA above the native button zone. |
| Landscape | 1200×628 | Link ads. Photo left, type right, or type only. |
| Carousel card | 1080×1080 | One symptom per card, product on the last card. |

Every asset: one symptom, one product, one price, one CTA, one yellow element. If it needs a second price, it's two ads.

| Trust | `/about`, `/how-it-works`, `/insights` | The studio, the process, three articles |
| Referral | `/refer` | The standing offer and form |

**Rule:** cold ads land on `/sprint` (or a sector page when sector-targeted). Never the homepage. Retargeting can use `/brand-build`, `/pricing` or a sector page.

### 11.2 The CTA

Primary CTA everywhere is **"Start a project"**, which opens `/start`. Product pages use the product's own button: "Book a sprint", "Start a brand build", "Start a subscription". Links can pre-select the product: `/start?product=sprint`, `/start?product=build`, `/start?product=subscription`.

The start form asks: what do you need (sprint, build, subscription, not sure), what kind of business (the six sectors plus other), team size, when (this month, next month, just looking), then business and contact details. Every submission is a lead. Nobody is rejected. Sprint, build and subscription go to a booking calendar; "not sure" and "just looking" get the free Brand Score quiz and a follow-up email.

### 11.3 Tracking and attribution

- Append UTMs to every ad link: `utm_source=meta`, `utm_medium=paid`, `utm_campaign=<campaign>`, `utm_content=<hook-id>`. The site captures them, with the click id, and passes them through to the lead record, the CRM and the booking.
- Referral or partner links use `?ref=<name>` and are captured the same way.
- Meta conversion event: **Lead** fires once per person, on form submit. Bookings fire **Schedule**. Optimise campaigns to Lead only.
- Page-level custom events exist for form start, form submit, checkout start and referral submit.

### 11.4 What happens after the click

1. Ad → `/sprint` → "Book a sprint" → `/start?product=sprint` (lands on step two) → details → booking calendar for a 15-minute sprint call. A follow-up email with the sprint details goes out immediately.
2. Or, when enabled: `/sprint` → "Pay £799 and book" → Stripe Checkout → `/sprint/thanks` → booking calendar. The team is notified and the lead is stored.
3. Build and subscription routes book a 30-minute intro call and send the matching follow-up email.

## 12. Campaign architecture

### 12.1 Objectives

| Stage | Objective | Audience | Creative | Destination |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold | Leads (Lead event) | UK, 28 to 60, business owners and founders; sector interest stacks for sector campaigns | Symptom hook → sprint | `/sprint` or `/for/<sector>` |
| Warm | Leads | Site visitors 30 days, Instagram engagers, video viewers | Proof-led: real work, the price on the page, the credit rule | `/brand-build`, `/pricing` |
| Hot | Leads | `/start` abandoners, `/pricing` visitors | Objection answers, testimonial, "the price is the price" | `/start?product=…` |
| Past clients | Traffic / Leads | Customer list | "Brands drift. Want it tightened up?" and the referral offer | `/sprint`, `/refer` |

### 12.2 The creative matrix

Build the set as hook × product × sector × format. Start with the proven hooks:

| Hook family | Product | Sector variants |
|---|---|---|
| Brand looks unclear / ten people made it | Identity sprint | All six, using sector-specific objects (menu, van, forecourt, clinic reception) |
| Homepage can't explain you | Homepage sprint | Property-finance and trades first (service businesses live by the homepage) |
| Deck doesn't match the website | Deck sprint | Property-finance, trades, "other" (B2B) |
| Business grown, brand hasn't | Brand Build | Hospitality and retail first (visible brands) |
| Design is always the thing that's late | Subscription | Warm and past-client audiences only |

Do not run the subscription hook cold. It has already been tested. It doesn't work.

### 12.3 Budget

£30 a day on Meta was the planned test budget, one hook family, one page, one event. Scale a hook only once cost per sprint enquiry is at or under £60 and lead-form completion is at or over 5 percent.

## 13. Brief template

Every brief handed to the design team should contain:

1. **Campaign and objective.** Cold / warm / hot / past client. Lead event.
2. **Audience.** Sector, geography (UK), who they are in one line, what they feel (§4.1).
3. **The symptom.** One of the four, in the buyer's words, or a sector variant.
4. **The product.** One. Price with +VAT. Timeframe. The one-line scope.
5. **Headline options.** Three, each under eight words, uppercase condensed, no exclamation marks.
6. **Body line.** One sentence. The fix and the price.
7. **Proof element.** A named client from §6.2 in the right sector, or a number from §6.1, or a testimonial from §6.3. Never invented.
8. **Image direction.** Which real work, from where (`/public/work/…` or the case study), how it's framed (card on black or full-bleed with scrim).
9. **The yellow element.** Which one thing is yellow.
10. **CTA.** "Book a sprint" / "Start a brand build" / "Start a project". Destination URL with UTMs.
11. **Formats.** From §10.7, with safe areas.
12. **Motion notes** if animated, from §10.6.
13. **Banned-list check.** Confirm nothing from §8.6 appears.
